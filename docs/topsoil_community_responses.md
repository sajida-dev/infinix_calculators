```text
This is a question every homeowner faces when starting a landscaping project! The short answer is: yes, topsoil is 100% worth it for the top 4 to 6 inches where plant roots feed, but fill dirt is much more cost-effective for deep structural grading below 8 inches.

Using topsoil deep underground is a waste of money because organic matter degrades and compresses over time, causing settled spots. On the flip side, planting grass directly into subsoil or fill dirt leads to patchy, starved lawns because subsoil lacks organic nutrients and microbial life.

If you're trying to figure out whether to order bulk truckloads or individual bags, bulk topsoil usually breaks even around 2 to 3 cubic yards (where truck delivery fees are offset by the per-yard savings).
```

**Topic**: Evaluating topsoil quality, texture, clumping, and yardage estimation.

### Copy-Paste Comment Text:
```text
Here are 4 quick checks to tell if your topsoil is good quality:

1. Color & Organic Matter: Good topsoil should be dark brown or rich black. Light brown or orange usually indicates high subsoil/clay content with low organic matter.
2. Squeeze Test: Dampen a handful and squeeze it. It should form a loose ball that crumbles easily when poked. If it forms a hard, sticky clay mass, it has poor drainage. If it falls apart like beach sand, it won't hold nutrients.
3. Debris Ratio: A good screened topsoil should have less than 5% wood chips, rocks, or un-composted debris.
4. Smell: Quality topsoil has a fresh, earthy forest floor aroma (actinomycetes bacteria), not a sour or anaerobic manure odor.

If you're topping up raised beds or prepping for new sod, aim for a 4-inch to 6-inch soil depth so roots can anchor properly.
```
